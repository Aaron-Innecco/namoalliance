Title IX: Amendments and Interpretation

The Process for Changing and Interpreting the Constitution

Title IX of the Constitution defines how the Meritocratic Republic of Naas may change or interpret its supreme legal document. This Title ensures that the Constitution remains stable while allowing careful and deliberate updates when necessary. Certified voters must understand this Title because constitutional amendments shape the future of the Republic and interpretation determines how laws are applied.

Amendments may be proposed by the President or by one third of the Assembly. This ensures that both executive leadership and representative bodies may initiate constitutional change. The President’s authority reflects the stability of the office, while the Assembly’s authority ensures democratic participation by certified voters.

Proposed amendments require approval by two thirds of the Assembly. This supermajority requirement prevents sudden or unstable changes. It ensures that amendments reflect broad national consensus rather than temporary political trends.

Constitutional interpretation is the responsibility of the President and the Assembly. Interpretation ensures that the Constitution is applied consistently and fairly. It allows the government to clarify meaning, resolve ambiguity, and ensure that laws align with constitutional principles.

Title IX establishes a balanced system for constitutional change. It protects the stability of the Republic while allowing careful adaptation. Certified voters must understand this Title to evaluate amendment proposals and interpret political decisions correctly.
